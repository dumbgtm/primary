**Short answer:** no. Google still handles most searches, search volume is still growing, and Google's AI answers are built from pages that rank. What is shrinking is the number of clicks you get from a ranking, especially for simple questions that AI now answers right on the results page. The part that's fading is the old habit of ranking a definition page and collecting the traffic.

## The longer answer: what the data says

People have been declaring SEO dead for a long time, which is why the take itself sits firmly in the Boring stage. Here's what has and hasn't changed.

**Search is still big and still growing.**

- Google had **73.7% of US desktop searches** in Q4 2025, according to SparkToro and Datos' analysis of 41 sites with search activity. ChatGPT had about **2.9%**, behind Amazon, Bing and YouTube. (This is desktop panel data only.)
- Alphabet's search revenue was **$63.3 billion in Q2 2026, up 17%** from a year earlier, and CEO Sundar Pichai said AI features are "driving Search query growth."
- ChatGPT is growing fast, with **900 million weekly active users** reported in February 2026. A lot of that usage is writing, coding and chatting, though, not looking things up.

**But each search sends fewer clicks.**

- In 2024, **58.5% of US Google searches** ended without a click to any website, according to SparkToro's zero-click study.
- Pew Research tracked real Google searches in March 2025. When an AI summary appeared, people clicked a regular result on **8%** of visits, versus **15%** when there wasn't one.
- Ahrefs looked at 300,000 keywords. For queries that show an AI Overview, the top result's click-through rate went from about **7.3% in December 2023 to 1.6% in December 2025**. Ahrefs reports this as a 58% drop compared with what it would have been without AI Overviews. The 7.3% to 1.6% figures are the clearer way to read it.

## What's dying and what isn't

| Type of SEO | Status in 2026 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **"What is X" and definition pages** | Struggling | AI Overviews answer these on the results page |
| **Generic how-to content** | Struggling | Same reason, and AI tools write this content cheaply |
| **Product, pricing and comparison pages** | Healthy | People still click to decide before they buy |
| **Local search** | Healthy | "Near me" searches lead to calls, visits and bookings |
| **Original data, research and tools** | Healthy | AI answers cite them, and people click to see the details |
| **Opinion and expertise** | Healthy | An AI summary can't replace a specific person's take |
| **Technical SEO basics** | Required | AI features use pages that are indexed and eligible to rank |

The pattern: if your page can be fully replaced by a three-sentence summary, AI will do that and the click goes away. If someone needs to see the page itself to make a decision or get something done, SEO still works.

## Why SEO matters for AI search too

Google's guidance for AI Overviews and AI Mode says there are no special optimizations needed beyond normal SEO, and that pages have to be indexed and eligible to appear with a snippet to be used. So the people saying "forget SEO, do AEO" have it backwards. AI search runs on the same foundation. We cover the differences in [AEO vs SEO vs GEO](/blog/aeo-vs-seo-vs-geo).

## What to do instead of panicking

1. **Check where your traffic comes from.** In Google Search Console, sort pages by clicks over the last 16 months. If informational pages are falling while product and comparison pages hold steady, you're seeing the pattern above.
2. **Move effort to pages that lead to a decision.** Comparisons, pricing, alternatives, use cases and local pages.
3. **Publish things AI can't summarize away.** Original numbers, tools, templates and real opinions.
4. **Write so you get quoted.** Put a direct answer at the top of each page and cite your sources, so AI answers can use you even when nobody clicks.
5. **Measure more than clicks.** Track whether you're mentioned in AI answers for the questions your buyers ask.

So is SEO dead? No. The cheap version that ranked thin articles for easy questions is fading. The version that helps people make decisions, and gets you cited by AI tools along the way, is still worth doing.
