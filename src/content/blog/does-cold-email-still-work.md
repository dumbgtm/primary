**Short answer:** yes, cold email still works in 2026, but the version that works looks different from the one most outbound tools sell. Small lists, specific reasons for reaching out and short sequences still get replies. Sending thousands of lightly personalized emails a day mostly gets you into spam folders, because inbox providers got much stricter in 2024 and 2025 and buyers got used to the patterns.

## What the numbers say

Nearly all cold email benchmarks come from companies that sell cold email software, based on their own users. They're useful for a rough baseline, as long as you remember who's publishing them.

| Benchmark | Source | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Average reply rate** | Instantly, 2025 platform data | 3.43% |
| **Average reply rate** | Saleshandy, 30M emails, Jul to Oct 2026 | 4.5% |
| **Top 10% of senders** | Instantly | 10.7%+ |
| **Lists under 50 people** | Woodpecker, via lemlist | 5.8% |
| **Lists over 1,000 people** | Woodpecker, via lemlist | 2.1% |
| **Replies that came from follow-ups** | Saleshandy | ~57% |

Two patterns show up in almost every dataset. Smaller, more targeted lists get more replies per email. And a big share of replies come from the first follow-up, so a one-email "sequence" leaves results on the table.

Open rates are missing from that table on purpose. Saleshandy calls open rate "one of the least trustworthy metrics in cold email," because privacy features and security scanners open emails automatically. Track replies and meetings instead.

## What changed

**Inbox rules got stricter.** In 2024, Google started requiring bulk senders to authenticate their domains, offer one-click unsubscribe on marketing email, and keep spam complaint rates below 0.1%. Senders that reach 0.3% lose access to Google's delivery support. Microsoft followed in May 2025 with authentication requirements (SPF, DKIM and DMARC) for anyone sending more than 5,000 emails a day to Outlook.com, Hotmail and Live addresses. Mail that doesn't comply goes to junk and can be rejected.

**Everyone got the same tools.** AI writing assistants and "AI SDRs" made it cheap to send thousands of personalized-looking emails. That pushed cold email into the Everywhere stage: the tactic still works, but buyers now recognize the template ("noticed you're scaling...") within a line or two. We wrote about where that leads in [Are AI SDRs worth it?](/blog/are-ai-sdrs-worth-it)

**Calls became more interesting by comparison.** With inboxes crowded, a lot of teams are adding phone back into their outbound. More on that in [Does cold calling still work?](/blog/does-cold-calling-still-work)

## Is cold email legal?

Generally yes, with rules. This is a summary, not legal advice.

- **United States (CAN-SPAM):** The FTC says the law "makes no exception for business-to-business email." You need accurate sender information, a subject line that matches the content, a valid postal address, a clear way to opt out, and you have to honor opt-outs within 10 business days. Penalties can reach $53,088 per email.
- **Canada (CASL):** Stricter than the US. Commercial email generally needs consent. Implied consent can apply when a person has conspicuously published their business email without saying they don't want unsolicited messages, and your message is relevant to their role. Penalties can reach $10 million per violation for a business.
- **EU and UK:** Separate rules apply under GDPR and local e-privacy laws. Check them before emailing people there.

## How to run cold email that still works

1. **Keep lists small and specific.** A few hundred well-chosen people with a shared problem beats 10,000 rows from a database.
2. **Give a real reason for this person.** One sentence about why you're contacting them specifically, based on something you know about their company or role.
3. **Write short emails.** A few sentences and one clear question. Saleshandy recommends first emails under 100 words.
4. **Send 2 to 4 follow-ups, then stop.** The first follow-up does a lot of the work. Long sequences mostly generate spam complaints.
5. **Protect your main domain.** Send from a separate domain, set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC, warm it up for a few weeks, and keep each inbox's daily volume low.
6. **Watch complaints, not opens.** Check your spam rate in Google Postmaster Tools and keep it under 0.1%.
7. **Pair it with another channel.** An email followed by a call or a LinkedIn message gives the person more than one way to recognize you.

So does cold email still work? Yes, for teams willing to send less and say something specific. The high-volume version is getting harder every year, and the inbox rules make sure of it.
